What you have just seen

1. Some verbs open. A noun can go inside or after: turn the power off, turn off the power.

2. When those verbs meet a pronoun (it, them), there is one place only: inside. Turn it off.

3. Some verbs never open. Noun or pronoun, the object comes after: came up with a sequence, came up with it.

Light inside, heavy last

On the last page: a noun has two places, a pronoun has one. Here is the reason, and it is one rule, not two.

1. One verb, an object that grows

The verb is set up. The object gets longer each time. Which version works?

2. Why

English keeps the heaviest thing for the end.

itlighter thanUPlighter thanthe new acquisition card from the supplier

It is the lightest word in English. One syllable, no stress, nothing new: you only say it when everybody already knows what you are talking about.

The small word is heavy. In these verbs it carries the stress: turn it OFF, back it UP, set it UP. You will work on that in the stress activity of this lesson.

So it cannot take the last place. The stressed word takes it, and it goes inside.

A long object is heavier than the small word, so the long object takes the end. A short noun weighs about the same, so both orders work.

Same thing for the other pronouns: back them up, call me back, pick us up.

3. French does it too

Replace the noun with a pronoun in both languages.

Je sauvegarde le fichier.

Back up the file.

In French the pronoun leaves the place of the noun and moves in front of the verb: je le sauvegarde.

In English it moves inside the verb: back it up.

You already know that a pronoun does not stay where the noun was. You only have to learn the new place.

The last small word

Some verbs open for a pronoun (back it up). Some never open (hack into it). You cannot hear the difference yet. But you can often see it.

1. Opens or stays shut?

Fourteen verbs from your deck, one at a time. Look at the last small word and decide. Guessing is fine: the pattern comes after.

2. The pattern

Read the last small word of each verb you have just sorted.

Last small wordWhat the verb doesFrom your deck
up, out, off, down, away, inIt opens. The pronoun goes inside.back it up, print it out, turn it off, shut it down, put them away, plug it in
into, with, of, for, to, throughIt never opens. The pronoun goes after.hack into it, come up with it, run out of it, sign up for it, opt in to it, run through it
onBoth. You have to learn these two.turn it on, but click on it

Why the second group never opens. Into, with, of, for, to are prepositions, and a preposition is glued to what follows it: hack [into the server], hack [into it]. Nothing can get between them, so nothing moves.

Two small words? The last one is always glue: come up [with it], run out [of it], sign up [for it]. A verb with two small words never opens.

How far you can trust this. It works for every verb in your deck. English has thousands more, and a few break it: you get off the bus, and you get off it.

For a new verb, a learner’s dictionary shows the place of the object. back sth up: it opens. hack into sth: it does not. (sth = something.)
