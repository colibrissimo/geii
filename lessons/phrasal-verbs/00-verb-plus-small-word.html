Verb + small word

Before the video. English has thousands of verbs made of a verb and a small word: turn off, back up, log in. They are called phrasal verbs. What are they, and how do you read them?

1. Two words in English, one in French

Read the sentence. Pick the French verb that means the same. One click per sentence.

Verb + small word = one verb, one meaning.

turn+off=éteindre

Very often, French says it with one verb. Read the two words together, never one by one.

2. Same verb, new small word

The verb is always turn. Only the small word changes. What does it mean here?

The small word carries the meaning.

Some small words come back again and again with the same idea:

up: more, or ready. Turn up the gain, power up the bench, boot up the PC, set up the router.

down: less, or stop. Turn down the volume, shut down the workstation.

on / off: power. Turn it on, turn it off.

3. Can you always guess?

Two more. Read the whole sentence.

No. Some phrasal verbs say what they do (plug in, print out). Some do not (figure out, run out of). When the two words make no sense one by one, the sentence tells you, and the deck lists them all.

What to keep

1. A phrasal verb is one verb in two or three words: turn off, come up with.

2. The small word changes the meaning: turn on, turn off, turn up, turn down.

3. You cannot always guess from the parts. Read the sentence.

You use some of them every day without knowing it. In tech, the verb came first and became a noun: back up → a backup, log in → a login, set up → the setup, plug in → a plugin.
