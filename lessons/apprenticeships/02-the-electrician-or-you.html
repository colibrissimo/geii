The electrician or you?

Nick, in the video, did an apprenticeship to become an electrician in the USA. At the IUT de Lille, you can do your B.U.T. GEII en alternance from Year 2, in all three parcours. Who does each sentence describe? Choose for all ten, then lock.

THE ELECTRICIANB.U.T. GEIIBOTH

1
If you choose this, you usually have one career path and it is not easy to change career.
Nick trains for one trade: electrician. A B.U.T. GEII is a broad degree: you can work as a technician or go on to an engineering school. Some of you may say both: that is worth discussing.
2
You get on-the-job training from experts, but you also have to study.
Nick works full-time and goes to school. In alternance, you alternate between the company and the IUT, and you are paid: you are an employee.
3
To be accepted, you must first take an aptitude test.
Nick took an aptitude test (0:37). For alternance at the IUT de Lille there is no test: you need to find a company that hires you.
4
You train to be one particular professional, without learning everything about the industry.
Nick trains to be an inside wireman. A B.U.T. GEII covers energy, automation and electronics, not one job only.
5
The main purpose is to gain practical experience.
True for both: you learn by doing real tasks in a real workplace.
6
You have a mentor who supports you and answers your questions.
In alternance you have a maître d'apprentissage in the company (your mentor) and a tutor at the IUT.
7
At the end, you become certified and can start working in your field.
Nick becomes a certified electrician. In alternance you get exactly the same B.U.T. diploma as full-time students.
8
It allows you to learn a trade: a skilled job where you usually work with your hands.
An electrician learns a trade. A B.U.T. GEII leads to technician and engineering jobs: hands-on, but not only a trade.
9
You study electronics, automation and energy, not the skills of one job only.
That is the B.U.T. GEII programme, in alternance or not. Nick learns the skills of one job.
10
Afterwards, you can go on to an engineering school.
Yes, and you can even do engineering school in alternance too. Nick's path leads straight to work as an electrician.

Talk. Which answer surprised you? Which one do you disagree with, and why?
