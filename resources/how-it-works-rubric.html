GEII Presentation Assessment Rubric

Presentation Overview

Duration:
2-3 minutes per student
Format:
Group presentations (3 or 4 students)
Topic:
"How it works": one component, tool or concept from your GEII coursework (one topic per group)
Components:
Presentation + Pronunciation + Q&A
Your track: Everyone starts in Track B. At your preparation session, you confirm Track B or ask to change to Track A or Track C. Your teacher makes the final decision. Every track can earn 20/20.

Track A Detailed Criteria (A1-A2)

1. Pronunciation Challenge 20%

Requirements: Correctly pronounce 5-8 technical terms. Word list must be submitted 1 week before presentation.

Standard: Words must be understandable (accent is not important).

Acceptable Pronunciation Examples:
  • microcontroller → "micro-con-tro-ler" (any comprehensible version)
  • algorithm → "algo-rithem" or "algo-rithm" (both OK)
  • resistor → "re-zis-tor" (understandable pronunciation)

3. Authenticity & Delivery 20%

Key Indicators:

  • Authenticity - Are you communicating or just reading?
  • Effort - Did you practice enough to avoid constant reading?
  • Engagement - Eye contact, gestures, showing you care

4. Content Accuracy 30%

What We're Checking:

  • Do you understand the basics of what you're presenting?
  • Are your technical facts correct?
  • Can you explain simple concepts accurately?
Engineering Content Examples:
  • Explaining what a breadboard is for and how its rows are connected
  • Describing what a multimeter measures: voltage, current and resistance
  • Explaining what happens in a circuit when you press a push button

Track B Detailed Criteria (B1-B2)

1. Pronunciation Challenge 20%

Requirements: Correctly pronounce 8-12 technical terms with proper word stress, OR read one complex technical sentence with appropriate intonation.

Standard: Near-accurate pronunciation expected.

Expected Standard Examples:
  • microcontroller → "micro-con-TROL-ler" (stress on TROL)
  • architecture → "AR-ki-tek-cher" (stress on AR)
  • signal processing → "SIG-nal PRO-cess-ing"

2. Q&A Session 40%

Question Types:

  • Analysis questions
  • Hypothetical scenarios
  • Application to context
  • Justification of choices

What Matters:

  • ✓ Can you explain WHY, not just WHAT?
  • ✓ Can you connect ideas?
  • ✓ Can you defend your choices?
  • ✓ Do you show critical thinking?
Example Question Types:
  • "Why does an LED need a resistor? What happens if you connect it without one?"
  • "Why do we measure voltage in parallel but current in series?"
  • "Why does a circuit need a common ground?"

3. Authenticity & Delivery 20%

Key Indicators:

  • Professionalism - Would this be acceptable in a workplace presentation?
  • Naturalness - Do you sound like you're talking or reciting?
  • Presence - Do you command attention and engage your audience?

4. Content Accuracy 20%

What We're Checking:

  • Do you understand WHY things work, not just WHAT they are?
  • Can you explain technical details?
  • Do you show critical thinking about the topic?
Expected Technical Depth:
  • Explaining why we choose one component and not another: a push button or a switch?
  • Describing what goes wrong when a component is used badly: an LED the wrong way round, a short circuit on a breadboard
  • Comparing analogue and digital signals with an example from your lab work

Track C Detailed Criteria (C1-C2)

1. Pronunciation Challenge 15%

Requirements: Correctly pronounce 10-15 complex technical/scientific terms with native-like stress and intonation. Must include at least 3 multi-syllable terms (4+ syllables).

Standard: Native-like pronunciation with natural connected speech.

Expected Standard Examples:
  • electromagnetic compatibility → "e-lek-tro-mag-NET-ik com-PAT-i-BIL-i-ty" (natural linking)
  • semiconductor → "SEM-i-con-DUC-tor" (primary and secondary stress)
  • Fourier transform → "FOOR-i-ay trans-FORM" (correct French pronunciation + English stress)

2. Q&A Session 45%

Question Types:

  • Critical analysis and evaluation
  • Complex hypothetical scenarios
  • Cross-disciplinary connections
  • Defence of methodology/approach
  • Spontaneous elaboration on unexpected aspects

What Matters:

  • ✓ Can you critically evaluate and not just describe?
  • ✓ Can you discuss implications and consequences?
  • ✓ Can you synthesise information from multiple sources/contexts?
  • ✓ Can you engage in spontaneous technical discussion?
Example Question Types:
  • "Why use a transistor as a switch and not a push button? What are the advantages and limits?"
  • "Your DC power supply shows the right voltage, but your circuit does not work. What could be wrong, and how would you check?"
  • "The real world is analogue, but most modern systems are digital. What do we gain, and what do we lose?"

3. Authenticity & Delivery 20%

Key Indicators:

  • Professional engineering standard - Comparable to industry conference presentations
  • Rhetorical skill - Strategic use of emphasis, pausing, signposting
  • Audience awareness - Adapts to audience reactions; engages effectively

4. Content Accuracy 20%

What We're Checking:

  • Can you critically evaluate technical approaches, not just describe them?
  • Do you understand limitations and trade-offs?
  • Can you contextualise within broader engineering/scientific frameworks?
  • Do you demonstrate independent critical thinking?
Expected Sophistication:
  • Evaluating the advantages and limits of your component compared with other solutions
  • Explaining what happens at the limits: too much current, the wrong voltage, a bad ground connection
  • Connecting your topic to other components and systems you have studied in GEII

Important Notes for Students

About Group Work

  • Each member will be individually assessed
  • All members should participate roughly equally
  • Coordinate your content but speak authentically
  • You'll each answer different questions in Q&A

About AI Use

I know you will use AI to help prepare. That's fine. But remember:
  • ✓ AI can write your content
  • ✗ AI cannot present for you
  • ✗ AI cannot answer my questions for you
  • ✗ AI cannot pronounce technical terms for you

Your grade depends on what YOU understand and can do, not what AI prepared.

About Preparation

  1. One week before: Submit your pronunciation word lists (individually)
  2. Record yourselves practising (not graded, but required)
  3. Understand your content - don't just memorise
  4. Practise answering questions about your topic
  5. Time your presentation - stay within 2-3 minutes per person

Red Flags (What Gets You a Low Grade):

  • ✗ Reading entirely from notes/slides
  • ✗ Cannot answer simple questions about your own topic
  • ✗ No evidence of practice (pronunciation disasters)
  • ✗ Clearly don't understand what you're saying
  • ✗ Going significantly over/under time
  • ✗ Unequal participation in group

Green Lights (What Gets You a Good Grade):

  • ✓ Practised pronunciation of technical terms
  • ✓ Can explain your topic in YOUR OWN WORDS
  • ✓ Show you understand, even if your English isn't perfect
  • ✓ Make eye contact and try to communicate
  • ✓ Answer questions with evidence of real understanding
  • ✓ All group members participate actively

Final Grade Calculation

Your final presentation grade = Sum of all components
