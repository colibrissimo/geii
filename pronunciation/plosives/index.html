Meet the puff

At the start of a stressed syllable, English p, t and k come with a small burst of air.

The paper test: hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth and say pot. The paper should move.

In French it does not move. That is the whole lesson.

With the puff

/pʰ/ /tʰ/ /kʰ/

Start of a stressed syllable. A small explosion of air before the vowel arrives.

Say it too gently and an English ear hears battery where you said pattery.

No puff

/sp/ /st/ /sk/

Straight after an S. No burst — the sound is clean and flat.

Identical to the French plosive. The only free gift in this lesson.

Notes

Why it is worth the trouble

English does not tell p from b mainly by voicing — it tells them apart by the puff. Drop the puff and your p lands in b territory.

Listen to both. The difference you hear at the start is air, not voice.

Where the rule does not apply

The puff needs a stressed syllable. In compu·ter and po·lice the first consonant is unstressed and the puff is weak or absent.

So the quiz sticks to the eight clean pairs, where the answer is never arguable.

The eight pairs

Same consonant, twice. On the left it puffs, on the right it does not. Click both. Hear the air appear and disappear.
